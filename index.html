Center for Smart Convergent Manufacturing Systems, RPI

Precision automated linear scaling system

2025

Project Manager: senior capstone for Howmet Aerospace

Howmet's saw operators were measuring aerospace billet with a handheld tape measure, accurate to about 1/16 in. Cuts came out underweight and the material could not be recovered. My team's job was to automate the measurement without replacing the saw.

  • Led an 11-person team across three subgroups as project manager, from problem definition through concept selection and prototype.
  • Held the design to a real spec: 0.001 in accuracy, 50 in of travel, under $1,500, and robust to operator error.
  • Selected between belt, ball-screw and track architectures with a QFD and weighted decision matrix: the screw system won at roughly $1,000 against $10,000 for the marginally more repeatable belt.
  • Built a working prototype: programmable FUYU linear guide, NEMA 23 stepper, Arduino control, an HMI, and a VL53L0X time-of-flight alternative path.

Controls & kinematics

DRAWbot: an arm that draws what you sketch

2025

Team of 4: Robotics course, RPI

We made a 5-DOF DOFBOT arm reproduce Microsoft Paint sketches on paper. An image goes in; OpenCV extracts and downsamples its contours; inverse kinematics turns every contour point into joint angles; and a spring-loaded Sharpie end-effector we designed and 3D-printed keeps the pen on the page while the arm draws.

  • Redefined the arm's product-of-exponentials model with a new tool frame for the pen, and solved IK per contour point with a Jacobian-inverse iterative method.
  • Constrained the base joint by arctangent per point so the pen stays perpendicular to the drawing plane across the whole page.
  • The spring-loaded, notched adapter free-floats the pen, keeping contact through surface variation without dragging friction.

Acrobot swing-up and balance: RL + adaptive LQR

2026

Adaptive Systems & Reinforcement Learning, RPI

The acrobot is a two-link pendulum actuated only at the elbow, an underactuated benchmark that is easy to state and hard to balance. Our system swings it up with a DQN policy, then hands off to my adaptive LQR to catch and hold it upright.

  • Built the stabilizer as a certainty-equivalence adaptive LQR in MATLAB: four parallel RLS estimators identify the discrete [A, B] model online (forgetting factor 0.98), and the LQR gain is re-solved from the estimates at every step.
  • Stabilizes the full nonlinear dynamics from perturbed initial conditions under a ±10 N·m torque limit.
  • Mapped the basin of attraction with a 400-simulation Monte Carlo sweep over initial angle and velocity perturbations.

Scientific computing

Wildfire spread simulator

2024 – 2025

Fire and Energy Laboratory, Georgia Southern

How fast a wildfire moves depends on how wind flows through vegetation, which depends on drag. I implemented a finite-volume immersed boundary method on a Julia Navier–Stokes solver to compute drag coefficients for rigid and flexible vegetation, simulating cylinder arrays at the Reynolds numbers wind actually reaches around pine needles (Re ≈ 140–307). The method validated against five published studies (Cₓ 1.29 vs 1.25–1.34), and its outputs feed fire-spread models like WFDS. Presented at the 2024 GSU Student Research Symposium.
