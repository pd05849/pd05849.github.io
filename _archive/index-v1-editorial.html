Engineering

Precision automated linear scaling system

Project Manager — senior capstone for Howmet Aerospace

2025

Howmet's saw operators were measuring aerospace billet with a handheld tape measure, accurate to about 1/16 in. Cuts came out underweight and the material could not be recovered into any other part number. My team's job was to automate the measurement without replacing the saw.

  • Led an 11-person team across three subgroups as project manager, from problem definition through concept selection and prototype.
  • Held the design to a real spec: 0.001 in accuracy, 50 in of travel, under $1,500, and robust to operator error — the previous system broke because operators advanced the billet before raising the contact plate.
  • Costed and compared three architectures — belt, ball screw and track actuator — and selected between them with a QFD and weighted decision matrix rather than by preference. The screw system won on 42 points to 38 and 37, mostly on accuracy, maintenance and cost: roughly $1,000 against $10,000 for the belt system that was marginally more repeatable.
  • Built a working prototype around a programmable FUYU linear guide with a NEMA 23 stepper, Arduino control and an HMI, and prototyped time-of-flight ranging with a VL53L0X as an alternative measurement path.
  • Wrote the safety case — physical guards, emergency stop, grounding and insulation — against the NSPE code of ethics.
The part that stuck: the cheaper design won. Being able to show why on a decision matrix, rather than argue for it, is what made that an engineering decision instead of an opinion.
